# Insurance Risk, Pricing & Profitability Analytics

**A SQL portfolio for measuring insurance portfolio profitability, evaluating whether premiums reflect underlying risk, identifying suspicious claims, and understanding how changing economic conditions affect claims costs.**

---

## Overview

Writing more policies does not automatically create a healthier insurance business.

Growth only creates value when the premiums collected are sufficient for the risk being accepted and the claims ultimately paid.

A portfolio can grow while profitability deteriorates underneath it.

That can happen when:

* Claims rise faster than premium
* High-risk policies are underpriced
* Premium does not reflect underwriting risk
* Fraudulent or suspicious claims increase loss exposure
* A small number of customers or products drive disproportionate claims
* Economic conditions push claims costs higher
* Reporting errors distort the true loss position of the portfolio

This repository analyzes those problems across a simulated insurance book containing **3,000 policies, 3,000 claims, customer records, underwriting decisions, fraud signals, and daily macroeconomic data from 2018 onward**.

The portfolio is organized around four management questions:

> **Are premiums covering claims?**

> **Are we pricing risk correctly?**

> **Which claims deserve investigation?**

> **Are economic conditions changing our claims exposure?**

Together, the projects move from basic insurance reporting toward **portfolio profitability, risk-adjusted pricing, claims control, and financial planning**.

# Projects

## 01. Claims Performance & Loss Ratio Intelligence

### Business Question

**Is the premium collected sufficient to cover the claims exposure being generated by the portfolio?**

Premium volume alone does not tell an insurer whether the book is economically healthy.

A product line can generate significant premium while claims consume most of it. A large customer can look commercially valuable until their claims history is considered. Portfolio-level averages can also hide weak products or policies underneath stronger performers.

This project evaluates claims performance across:

* Total Premium
* Total Claims
* Loss Ratio
* Policy-Level Claims Performance
* Customer-Level Claims Performance
* Product-Line Profitability
* Claims Concentration

At the center of the analysis is the loss ratio:

```text
Loss Ratio
=
Claims Incurred
───────────────
Premium Earned
× 100
```

This connects underwriting revenue directly to claims exposure.

### Critical Correction

The original analysis joined policies directly to claims and then aggregated premium.

Because one policy can have multiple claims, its premium could appear multiple times after the join.

A policy with:

```text
Premium: $4,000
Claims: 3
```

could contribute:

```text
$4,000 × 3 = $12,000
```

to an aggregated premium calculation even though only $4,000 of premium existed.

That inflates premium and can make loss ratios look healthier than they really are.

The corrected model aggregates claims at the appropriate grain before comparing them with policy premium.

### Business Outcome

The analysis helps insurance finance and portfolio teams answer:

> **Which parts of the book are generating enough premium for the claims exposure they create, and which require pricing, underwriting, or portfolio review?**

## 02. Underwriting Risk, Pricing & Premium Intelligence

### Business Question

**Does the premium being charged reflect the risk the insurer is actually accepting?**

Insurance pricing is fundamentally a risk-transfer decision.

Two policies with similar coverage should not automatically receive similar pricing if one carries materially greater expected loss exposure.

This project connects underwriting risk with:

* Current Premium
* Claims Experience
* Risk Classification
* Customer Exposure
* Pricing Adequacy

The system includes:

* Risk-Based Claims Comparison
* Pricing Adequacy Analysis
* Mispricing Detection
* Premium Review Classification
* Risk Segmentation

The objective is to identify situations where:

> **Risk and price are moving in different directions.**

A high-risk policy with weak premium may represent underpricing.

A low-risk policy carrying disproportionately high premium may create competitiveness or retention concerns.

---

### Premium Review Engine

Policies are converted into review categories based on their risk and pricing characteristics.

The purpose is not to automatically set an insurance premium from SQL.

It is to create a **pricing review queue**.

The output helps identify policies requiring:

* Pricing Review
* Underwriting Review
* Claims History Review
* Retention Review
* No Immediate Action

This converts underwriting data from a static score into something commercial teams can act on.

---

### Critical Corrections

Two counting problems were identified in the original analysis.

Both resulted from counting rows remaining after a `LEFT JOIN` rather than counting the actual business entity being measured.

This distinction matters because:

```text
Joined Rows ≠ Policies
Joined Rows ≠ Customers
Joined Rows ≠ Claims
```

unless the analytical grain guarantees that relationship.

The corrected analysis counts the intended entity explicitly, preventing claims history from artificially inflating policy or customer counts.

### Business Outcome

The project helps underwriting and pricing teams answer:

> **Where are we accepting more risk than the premium appears to justify, and which policies should be reviewed before that mispricing becomes future claims loss?**

## 03. Claims Fraud Detection & Investigation Intelligence

### Business Question

**Which claims show enough abnormal behavior to deserve investigation first?**

Fraud creates a different type of insurance loss.

It is not simply a question of how many claims exist.

The operational problem is prioritization.

An investigation team may have thousands of claims but limited capacity. Reviewing every claim with the same level of attention wastes investigative resources.

This project combines claims information with fraud signals to produce a ranked investigation view based on:

* Anomaly Score
* Fraud Signal
* Fraud Reason
* Claim Characteristics
* Geographic Concentration

The goal is to move from:

> **"Which claims could be fraudulent?"**

to:

> **"Which claims carry the strongest available fraud signals and should investigators review first?"**

---

### Investigation Watchlist

Claims exceeding the defined anomaly threshold are surfaced for review.

In this simulated portfolio, approximately:

> **1 in 5 claims**

crosses the anomaly threshold used by the model.

The confirmed fraud rate is approximately:

> **1 in 10 claims**

Those numbers should not be treated as interchangeable.

An anomaly flag is a screening signal.

It is not proof of fraud.

That distinction matters because the purpose of the model is to prioritize investigation, not automatically reject claims.

---

### Geographic Fraud Intelligence

Confirmed fraud is also evaluated geographically.

This helps identify whether suspicious or confirmed activity is concentrated in particular areas rather than evenly distributed across the portfolio.

Geographic concentration can support:

* Investigation Prioritization
* Underwriting Review
* Claims Process Review
* Regional Risk Monitoring

It should not, on its own, be treated as evidence that an individual claim is fraudulent.

### Business Outcome

The analysis gives claims teams a structured watchlist so limited investigation capacity can be directed toward claims with stronger available fraud signals.

## 04. Macroeconomic Conditions & Claims Cost Intelligence

### Business Question

**Are changing economic conditions showing up in the cost of claims?**

Insurance portfolios do not operate in isolation.

Inflation can increase repair and replacement costs.

Interest rates can change financial conditions.

Unemployment may influence customer behavior and portfolio risk.

This project examines how claims costs change alongside:

* Inflation
* Unemployment
* Interest Rates

from 2018 onward.

The objective is to give finance and risk teams additional context around claims-cost movement.

If claims severity increases during a period of rising inflation, that relationship may deserve deeper investigation when pricing assumptions and financial forecasts are reviewed.

The analysis identifies association.

It does not assume that macroeconomic movement alone caused a change in claims costs.

---

### Critical Correction

The original analysis connected claims directly to daily macroeconomic observations using year.

That created a grain mismatch.

Conceptually:

```text
One Claim in 2022
        |
        ↓
Every Daily Economic Record in 2022
```

A single claim could therefore appear hundreds of times.

Across an entire claims portfolio, this creates substantial row multiplication and can distort the resulting analysis.

The corrected approach first aggregates the daily economic data to the appropriate yearly level.

```text
DAILY MACRO DATA
       |
       ↓
YEARLY ECONOMIC CONDITIONS
       |
       ↓
JOIN TO YEARLY CLAIMS PERFORMANCE
```

The comparison now occurs between datasets at compatible analytical grains.

### Business Outcome

The project gives insurance finance and risk teams a cleaner basis for asking:

> **Are changes in the wider economic environment occurring alongside changes in claims cost, and should those movements inform future pricing, reserving, or planning discussions?**

The projects are intentionally separate because each supports a different insurance decision.

                         ↓
                     CLAIMS

The insurer's job is not simply to maximize premium.

It is to make sure the premium collected is appropriate for the risk accepted and the losses ultimately generated.

# Data Quality & Analytical Validation

A major part of this portfolio is not simply calculating KPIs.

It is verifying that the SQL represents the underlying insurance process correctly.

The scripts were reviewed for:

* Analytical Grain
* One-to-Many Relationships
* Join Cardinality
* Duplicate Exposure
* Entity Counting
* Metric Definitions
* Aggregation Logic
* Time-Grain Alignment

Several problems identified during review shared the same underlying cause:

> **Values from the "one" side of a one-to-many relationship were being aggregated after joining to the "many" side.**

This appeared in different forms across the portfolio.

### Policies → Claims

One policy can have multiple claims.

Premium should not multiply because claim count increases.

### Claims → Daily Economic Data

One year contains hundreds of daily economic observations.

Claims should not multiply because the economic dataset has a finer time grain.

### Joined Rows → Entity Counts

A joined row count should not automatically be interpreted as a count of customers, policies, or claims.

These corrections matter because insurance metrics can look perfectly reasonable while being materially wrong.

A query executing successfully is not evidence that the financial logic is correct.

# What This Portfolio Demonstrates

These four projects use the same insurance environment, but each addresses a different part of the economics of running an insurance book.

**Claims Performance** asks whether premium is sufficient for the claims being generated.

**Underwriting & Pricing** asks whether the business is charging appropriately for the risk it accepts.

**Fraud Intelligence** asks where abnormal claims activity deserves investigation before additional loss is paid.

**Macroeconomic Claims Intelligence** asks whether changes outside the insurer are occurring alongside changes in claims costs.

Together, they address four connected questions:

> **Are we collecting enough premium?**

> **Are we pricing the risk correctly?**

> **Are we controlling avoidable claims loss?**

> **Are external conditions changing the economics of the book?**

The repository therefore represents more than four SQL analyses.

It demonstrates how insurance data can be turned into a connected **risk, pricing, claims, and profitability decision framework** for managing the financial health of an insurance portfolio.
